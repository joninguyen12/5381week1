Eevee (National Pokédex #133) is a brown, quadruped “Evolution Pokémon” that makes its home in urban environments.  It belongs to the ground egg group, is not a baby, legendary, or mythical Pokémon, and is fairly easy to catch with a capture rate of 45.  

Eevee’s most notable trait is its ability to evolve into a whole family of related species.  According to the Pokédex, it can branch into Vaporeon, Jolteon, Flareon, Espeon, Umbreon, Leafeon, Glaceon, and Sylveon — listed in that order in the official data.  The English flavor text from many game versions consistently highlights its unstable genetic makeup: several entries note that its DNA is irregular and can mutate when exposed to radiation from elemental stones, allowing it to adapt smoothly to different surroundings.  Because the descriptions come from multiple game releases, the exact wording varies, but the core idea — an adaptable, genetically flexible creature — remains the same.  

Overall, Eevee is portrayed as a rare yet approachable Pokémon whose unique biology lets it thrive in city settings and shift into many distinct forms, making it a favorite among trainers who enjoy exploring evolutionary possibilities.
